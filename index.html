Biogeochemical Geoarchaeology Research Group

Department of Applied Geology, Indian Institute of Technology (ISM) Dhanbad

Established in 2026, BASIL is a young and growing research group in the Department of Applied Geology at IIT (ISM) Dhanbad. We work at the intersection of Organic Geochemistry, Quaternary Science, and Geoarchaeology. Our goal is to read molecular signatures preserved in sediments and archaeological deposits to reconstruct deep environmental and human histories, with a primary focus on South Asia and connections to global patterns.

About the Group

The Questions We Ask

Understanding how past climates shifted, vegetation transformed, and human communities adapted offers critical lessons for navigating today’s environmental challenges. Our research addresses three broad questions:

01

How did climate and ecosystems change over the Quaternary?

We use leaf-wax n-alkanes, macro-charcoal, and compound-specific isotope analysis (δ13C, δ2H) to track monsoon variability and vegetation dynamics.

02

How did humans interact with and modify these environments?

We investigate prehistoric fire use, dietary practices, and migration patterns in archaeological contexts.

03

What can deep-time perspectives tell us about environmental resilience?

We draw on long-term human–environment records to inform questions of sustainability and adaptation.

The lab is equipped with newly installed GC-MS infrastructure supported by ANRF funding and a live rain gauge for monsoon isotopic monitoring, a department-supported micromorphology setup and micro- and macro-charcoal analyses. We are actively building capacity for biomarker and isotope-based geoarchaeological research. Led by Dr. Deepak Kumar Jha, BASIL is expanding its team of doctoral researchers, students, and collaborators. See our facilities

Open to Collaboration

The questions we ask are global

As a new group, we welcome collaboration from anywhere in the world. Whether you have a new research idea, a project proposal, a joint funding opportunity, or simply a shared curiosity about the deep human–environment past, we are open to exploring it together. If your interests align broadly with geoarchaeology, palaeoclimatology, organic geochemistry, or human–environment interactions, we would love to hear from you.
